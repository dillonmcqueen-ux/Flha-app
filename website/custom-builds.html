Not a form builder

A form asks questions. A custom build runs the process.

Anyone can sell you a drag-and-drop form. A custom build is a real piece of software with your logic in it: what triggers, what blocks, what alerts, who gets notified, and what it does with the answer afterwards.

Six real ones

None of these exist in any off-the-shelf tool.

These are not features on a roadmap. They are the kind of one-off, specific thing a real company asks for, and exactly the kind of thing a custom build is for. If yours is not here, that is normal. Most of them are not.

Excavation contractor

Locate ticket tracker

Every utility locate request in one place with its ticket number, the dig it covers, and the date it goes stale. The FLHA for that dig checks the ticket before the machine moves, and flags the supervisor if the locate has expired or never came back.

Today: a pile of faxes in the truck and hoping the right one is on top.
Concrete contractor

Pour record and cylinder tracker

Every pour logged with the mix, the truck tickets, slump and air results, and which part of the structure it went into. Test cylinders get a break date, and the system reminds you before the lab result is overdue instead of when the engineer asks.

Today: a binder, a phone full of photos, and somebody's memory.
Aggregate hauler

Scale ticket reconciliation

Drivers photograph the scale ticket at the pit. Loads total by truck, by day, by pit. At month end you see what you hauled beside what you got paid for, and every gap is a line item with a ticket number attached.

Today: an envelope of paper tickets and a fight at the end of the month.
Anyone with fluids on site

Spill and release log

Product, volume, containment, photos, logged from the field the moment it happens. The reportable-quantity threshold and the regulator notification clock start automatically, so nobody has to remember what the limit was or when the window closes.

Today: a phone call to the office and a hope that somebody wrote it down.
Any company running trucks

Road ban and axle weight check

Current ban percentage by route, and the legal load for each specific unit at that level. Dispatch checks one screen before a truck leaves instead of three, and the answer is per unit, not a general rule somebody has to do math on.

Today: a screenshot in a group chat and a guess.
Mechanical and electrical trades

Tool crib sign-out

Who has the plate tamper, which truck it went out on, when it is due back, and what it is worth. Overdue items show up on the supervisor's dashboard, so a missing tool is a notification instead of a discovery six weeks later.

Today: a whiteboard nobody updates and a replacement invoice.

Six examples, not a menu. The point is that they are all different. The one you are picturing right now is probably closer to buildable than you think.

How it actually gets built

Tailored to what you need, not what I guessed.

I do not hand you a finished thing and tell you that is how it works now. You tell me the process, in whatever form it is in today, and I build it. Then we go back and forth until it is actually the process you run, not my interpretation of it.

Don't like where a button is? Say so, and it moves. That is a change inside your account, not a support ticket that goes into a queue behind four hundred other companies.

Need one process to read off another? The inspection that flags a machine should be able to open the work order. The locate ticket should be able to stop the dig. Say what needs to talk to what, and it gets scoped as part of the build.

Realised halfway through that you actually need it to work differently? That is normal, and it is why scoping happens with you instead of at you. Things change once you see the real thing on a real phone.

You are not buying a seat on a platform and hoping the roadmap eventually gets to you. You are working directly with the person who writes the code, and it is finished when you say it matches how your company runs.

The process

Four steps from headache to something you can log into.

01

Tell us what's broken

No spec document required. Describe the process, the form, or the headache in plain language. A voice note, an email or a phone call works fine.

02

We scope it together

You get a plain-language breakdown of exactly what would get built and a fixed quote before any work starts. No hourly rate, no surprises, no change-order games.

03

It gets built into your account

Not a separate app and not a separate login. It shows up inside FORA using the same roster and dashboard your crew already knows, and it plugs into the same Brain as everything else.

04

Then we fix what isn't right

You use it on a real site with a real crew. What does not fit gets changed. That part is the job, not an extra.

What it costs

The login, the database, the roster, the PDF exports, the mobile interface, the multi-tenant security: all of it is already built and already running. You pay for the part that is specific to your company, which is usually a lot smaller than it sounds.
