Not a form builder

A form asks questions. A custom build runs the process.

Anyone can sell you a drag-and-drop form. A custom build is a real piece of software with your logic in it: what triggers, what blocks, what alerts, who gets notified, and what it does with the answer afterwards.

QuestionA template builderA FORA custom build
Where does it live?Another app, another loginInside your FORA account, next to everything else
Who fills it out?Whoever remembers the app existsYour existing roster, on the menu they already use
Can it stop a job?No, it collects answersYes. It can block, flag, or escalate on your rules
Can it read your other records?NoYes, that is the point. Scoped as part of the build
Who decides how it works?Whatever the template allowsYou do, and it gets changed until it matches
Example one: you already have a system

A documentation portal. Turn what you already run into one login.

You're not starting from nothing. You've got forms, checklists and processes that already work, they just live on paper, in a text thread, or in whoever's head remembers the exceptions. A documentation portal doesn't reinvent any of that. It rebuilds every form you already use as a digital one, cross-referenced against your fleet, roster and SOPs wherever it matters.

  • Every existing form rebuilt digital, no redesign of how you work
  • One login for the whole crew, same roster as the rest of FORA
  • Cross-referenced against your other records automatically
  • Digital reports and the follow-up tasks that used to need a person to chase

A company app, wired into what you already pay for.

Some processes don't just need a digital form, they need to move data in and out of the other software you already run: your accounting package, a supplier's ordering system, a dispatch tool, whatever your office depends on. A company app is built with that API integration as part of the job, so the numbers move on their own instead of getting re-typed twice.

  • No more re-keying the same numbers into two systems
  • Custom workflows and notifications built around how your office actually runs
  • Whatever else "an app for your company" turns out to mean once we scope it

Example three: there's no ceiling

A full multi-site dispatch and scheduling command center.

This is what it looks like when a custom build stops being an add-on and becomes the operation's nerve center. Every crew and every machine, across every site, scheduled in real time. Book an excavator twice by accident and it gets flagged before anyone shows up short. Flag a machine for maintenance and dispatch sees it needs reassigning before the truck even leaves the yard.

  • Real-time crew and equipment scheduling across every site at once
  • Double-booking and conflicts flagged before they become a problem on site
  • A maintenance flag automatically pulls a machine out of the schedule
  • Built on the same roster, equipment list and Brain as everything else, not a separate system

The process

Four steps from headache to something you can log into.

01

Tell us what's broken

No spec document required. Describe the process, the form, or the headache in plain language. A voice note, an email or a phone call works fine.

02

We scope it together

You get a plain-language breakdown of exactly what would get built and a fixed quote before any work starts. No hourly rate, no surprises, no change-order games.

03

It gets built into your account

Not a separate app and not a separate login. It shows up inside FORA using the same roster and dashboard your crew already knows, and it plugs into the same Brain as everything else.

04

Then we fix what isn't right

You use it on a real site with a real crew. What does not fit gets changed. That part is the job, not an extra.
