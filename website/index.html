Why FORA is different

It's not a form, and it's not just software that sits still.

01

Grounded in your company, not the internet

Most "AI safety" tools generate generic, plausible-sounding output. FORA cross-references your company's own SOPs and equipment — the result reflects how your site actually operates.

02

Built for the glove, not the desk

Voice-first entry means a worker can generate a complete document standing in the field, phone in a dusty hand, without typing a paragraph.

03

One tool, five forms — and anything else you need

No stack of disconnected apps for inspections, toolbox talks, and incident reports. Everything a crew files lives in the same place — and when you need something more, Custom Builds extend it, on equal footing with the Big 5.

04

Multi-tenant isolation from day one

Every company's data is walled off from every other's at the access-control layer — not a setting you have to remember to turn on.

05

It learns your company over time

FORA's Brain starts with your SOPs and equipment at onboarding, then keeps learning from every FLHA edit, toolbox talk, and incident your crew actually files — so documents get more specific to your company the longer you use it. See how the Brain works →

Who's behind FORA

AI-assisted software. A real person behind it.

Fifteen years running heavy equipment on industrial and construction sites in Central Alberta. That's the resume line that actually matters here, because it's also where I learned the problem FORA solves. I know exactly what it's like to start a shift filling out the same FLHA information: same site, same hazards, same crew names, over and over, form after form, because nothing on paper ever talked to anything else. That's not a pain point I read about in a case study. That's how I spent a lot of mornings.

About four years ago I started teaching myself software, mostly through self-paced online courses on nights and days off, the same way I originally learned to run equipment: hands on, figure it out, get better by doing it again. FORA is what came out of that, and it's the most successful thing I've built so far. I'm not saying the bar was in orbit, but I'll take the win.

Call it what you want. Freelance digital solutions guy. Heavy equipment operator with a laptop habit. I won't pretend the jump from running equipment to writing software makes me a "traditional" tech founder, because it doesn't, and dressing it up as something it isn't would insult both jobs. What it does mean is FORA wasn't designed by someone guessing at what a job site needs. It was designed by someone who's filled out these forms in a truck cab, knows exactly why crews skip the steps that feel pointless, and is still hunting down new tools and processes to make it sharper.

I'm also an insured, registered business, not a guy running this out of a garage on good vibes. Every document FORA generates is AI-assisted. That's not a secret, and it's not something I'm hiding. But FORA isn't a venture backed platform run by a support queue on the other side of the planet. It's built and supported by one real person, based in Central Alberta, same part of the world as a lot of the crews using it. Email forafieldsolutions@gmail.com, or ask about a Custom Build, and you're talking straight to the person who builds it. No support queue, no offshore contractor. FORA's the biggest thing I've built. I'm already looking for what's next.
