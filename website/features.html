The field side

What a worker sees on their phone.

One menu, grouped into Safety, Equipment and General. No training session. Most workers file their first document without anyone showing them how.

Field Level Hazard Assessment

Under a minute

"Digging a trench near the east fence line" is a complete input. That one sentence is enough to produce a full, signed FLHA.

  • Voice or text entry, no form to tab through
  • Cross-referenced against your own SOPs and equipment list
  • Returns hazards, controls, required PPE and risk ratings
  • Signed in the field, exported as a clean PDF
  • Autosaves as a draft so a dropped call does not lose it

Toolbox Talk

Ready to present

Pick a topic and get a 5 to 10 minute talk a supervisor can walk straight into with the crew, no prep.

  • Written around your operations, not a stock topic off a list
  • Tied to the work actually happening that day
  • Attendance captured with the record
  • Filed and exportable like every other document

Incident Report

Regulator ready

Give it the raw details in whatever order they come out. It comes back as a formal, structured report.

  • Photo attachments stored against the report
  • Structured enough to hand to a regulator or insurer
  • Nothing gets lost because the person writing it was rattled

Near Miss Report

Catches patterns

A quick description of what almost happened becomes a clean report in well under a minute.

  • Low enough friction that the small ones actually get filed
  • The pattern shows up before it becomes an incident
  • Rolls into safety analytics automatically

Equipment Inspection

Per machine type

Pick the machine and get the pre-use checklist built for that exact type of equipment.

The Brain

Why your FLHAs get better and a competitor's don't.

Every account gets its own Brain on day one. Not a chatbot with your logo on it. A memory built only from your own documentation.

Underneath, it's a purpose-built AI reading your actual SOPs and equipment list, not a general chatbot guessing at your industry. Give it one sentence about the job and it cross-references that against your own documentation to hand back a complete, compliant document, hazards, controls, PPE and risk ratings included, in under a minute. Every genuine correction a supervisor makes gets folded back in, which is how the Brain actually gets built: not a one-time upload, but a memory of your operation that keeps sharpening itself. The point of the edge technology underneath all of it is that none of this requires the person filing the document to be any good with software. If a guy on your crew still has his login taped to the dash because typing was never his thing, he can talk into his phone and get back a document that would hold up to an auditor, faster than he could dig the binder out of the truck.
