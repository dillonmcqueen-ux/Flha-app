## Mid-market opportunity notes

**Photo-hazard-scanning is now a crowded sub-category, not open ground.** Two more live products surfaced this week: **OSHA Scan** (free/cheap, scans one photo, cites the specific OSHA standard, gives a compliance score and fix suggestions) and **DroneDeploy Safety AI** (enterprise/drone-scale, processes thousands of images weekly against OSHA 1910/1926). Combined with last week's Site Safety AI, that's three competitors already doing a version of the idea flagged two weeks ago. This sharpens rather than kills the idea: a bare photo scanner is already commoditized (one option is free), but *none* of these three tie hazard detection to a company's own SOPs the way FORA's document generation already does. The differentiated move is integration into the existing generation flow, not a standalone scanner feature. ([source](https://oshascan.com/), [source](https://www.dronedeploy.com/product/safety-ai))

## Out-of-the-box recommendation

**A short, plain-language "how FORA handles your data and AI" page.** Construction-sector concern about AI tools' privacy/security rose from 22% to 30% between 2025 and 2026 even as adoption grew from roughly half to two-thirds of professionals — trust is becoming a real evaluation criterion, and Mitti is already messaging on it directly ("we don't train on your data," flagged two weeks ago). FORA merged a real batch of security work this week (CSP/HSTS headers, an IDOR fix, login throttling, error-leak fix, a critical dependency bump) that a buyer will never see unless someone tells them. *Effort: low* — this is a documentation/marketing-copy task, not a build, and the underlying work is already done. ([source](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/latest/rising-privacy-and-security-concerns-hindering-ai-adoption-in-construction-industry-18-08-2026/))
